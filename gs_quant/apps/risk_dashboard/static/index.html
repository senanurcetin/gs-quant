      Definitions and assumptions
        Value at risk (VaR)
        The one period return that is not expected to be breached at the chosen confidence level. Losses are negative.
        Expected shortfall (ES)
        The average return on the periods that breach the VaR. Never better than the VaR.
        Breach
        A period whose return is worse than the VaR forecast made before it, from the previous window of returns.
        Kupiec test
        Tests the number of breaches against the confidence level. Rejects too many (risk understated) and too few (overstated).
        Christoffersen independence test
        Tests whether a breach makes another more likely the next period, i.e. whether they cluster.
        Conditional coverage
        Kupiec and independence combined.
        Basel traffic light
        Green, yellow or red from the binomial probability of seeing this many breaches or fewer if the model were right (95% and 99.99% cut-offs).
        Stress window
        The worst compounded return over any 1, 5 or 20 consecutive periods in the data, with its dates. It is what actually happened, not a forecast.
        Share of volatility
        Each asset's weight times its covariance with the portfolio, as a fraction of the portfolio variance. The shares add up to 100%.
        Share of expected shortfall
        The weight times the asset's average return on the periods when the portfolio was in its tail (worse than its VaR). Adds up to 100%.
        Diversification ratio
        The weighted average volatility of the assets divided by the volatility of the portfolio. 1 means no diversification.
        QQ plot
        Sample quantiles, in standard deviations, against normal quantiles. Points on the dashed line mean normal tails; points below it in the left tail mean fatter losses than a normal distribution allows.
